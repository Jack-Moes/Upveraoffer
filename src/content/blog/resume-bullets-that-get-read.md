A recruiter gives your résumé somewhere between six and twenty seconds on the first pass. In that window they are not reading — they are scanning for evidence that you have done the thing they need done.

Most bullets fail that scan for one reason: they describe a **responsibility** rather than a **result**.

## The pattern that fails

> Responsible for maintaining the backend API and working with the frontend team.

There is no information here. It describes a role, not a person. Any of the two hundred other applicants for the same job could have written it.

## The pattern that works

A bullet that survives the scan usually contains three things:

1. **The action you took** — a specific, concrete verb
2. **The mechanism** — how you did it, in enough detail to be credible
3. **The change** — what was different afterwards

Rewritten:

> Cut p95 API latency from 840 ms to 190 ms by replacing N+1 ORM queries with batched loaders, unblocking the mobile team's offline-first release.

Same job. Same work. Completely different signal.

## But I don't have numbers

Most people have more numbers than they think. You are not looking for a finance-grade metric — you are looking for *any* quantity that establishes scale or change:

- How many users, requests, records, or dollars did it touch?
- How long did it take before, and how long after?
- How many people were on the team? How many did you coordinate?
- How often did the thing break before? How often after?

And when there genuinely is no number, scale still helps:

> Migrated a twelve-year-old billing service to a new payments provider with no customer-visible downtime.

No metric. Still specific. Still credible.

## The honesty rule

Do not invent numbers. It is tempting, it is common, and it collapses the moment an interviewer asks a follow-up question — which they will, because specific claims invite specific questions. A vague bullet you can defend beats a spectacular one you cannot.

## A quick audit

Go through your résumé and mark every bullet with one of three letters:

- **R** — describes a responsibility
- **A** — describes an action with no outcome
- **C** — describes a change

Count them. If more than a third are R, that is why you are not hearing back. Start by rewriting those.
