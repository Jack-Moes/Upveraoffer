A recruiter gives your résumé somewhere between six and twenty seconds on the first pass. In that window they’re not really reading. They’re scanning for evidence that you’ve done the thing they need done.

Most bullets fail that scan for one reason: they describe a **responsibility** instead of a **result**.

## The pattern that fails

> Responsible for maintaining the backend API and working with the frontend team.

There’s nothing here. It describes a role, not a person. Any of the two hundred other applicants could have written the same line.

## The pattern that works

A bullet that survives the scan usually has three things in it:

1. **The action you took**, in a specific verb
2. **The mechanism**, in enough detail to be believable
3. **The change**, meaning what was different afterwards

Rewritten:

> Cut p95 API latency from 840 ms to 190 ms by replacing N+1 ORM queries with batched loaders, which unblocked the mobile team’s offline-first release.

Same job. Same work. Completely different signal.

## "But I don’t have numbers"

You probably have more than you think. You’re not looking for a finance-grade metric, just any quantity that shows scale or change:

- How many users, requests, records or dollars did it touch?
- How long did it take before, and how long after?
- How many people were on the team? How many did you coordinate?
- How often did it break before? How often after?

And when there genuinely isn’t a number, scale still does the work:

> Migrated a twelve-year-old billing service to a new payments provider with no customer-visible downtime.

No metric in there. Still specific. Still believable.

## Don’t make them up

It’s tempting, it’s common, and it falls apart the moment an interviewer asks a follow-up question. Which they will, because specific claims invite specific questions. A vague bullet you can defend beats a spectacular one you can’t.

## A quick audit

Go through your résumé and mark every bullet with one of three letters:

- **R** if it describes a responsibility
- **A** if it describes an action with no outcome
- **C** if it describes a change

Now count them. If more than a third are R, that’s why you’re not hearing back. Start there.
