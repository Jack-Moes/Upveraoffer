## Why this role exists

We coach people through job searches, and right now most of that coaching runs on
spreadsheets, calendar links, and manual review. It works, and it does not scale.

We are hiring a senior backend engineer to build the platform underneath it: a
coding-assessment engine that runs candidate submissions safely, a scheduling and
session pipeline that removes the coordination overhead from every engagement, and
the data model that finally lets us answer the question we care most about —
**which parts of our coaching actually change outcomes.**

This is the first senior backend hire. You will be setting patterns rather than
following them.

## What you will do

- **Design and build the assessment engine.** Untrusted code execution, sandboxing,
  resource limits, deterministic grading, and a submission pipeline that stays
  honest under time pressure.
- **Own the scheduling and session pipeline.** Booking, reminders, recording
  handoff, and the feedback artifacts that come out of every session.
- **Model the outcome data.** Build the schema and the reporting that connects what
  we did for a client to what happened in their search.
- **Set the engineering foundations.** Deployment, observability, testing strategy,
  and on-call expectations for a team that does not have them yet.
- **Handle personal data carefully.** Résumés, recordings, and job search activity
  are sensitive. You will own retention, access control, and deletion as real
  features, not as a checkbox.
- **Review and mentor.** You are the senior engineer; the quality bar is partly
  your responsibility.

## What we are looking for

- Five or more years building and operating backend systems in production — not
  just writing services, but keeping them alive.
- Strong command of at least one of Python, Go, TypeScript/Node, or Java, and
  comfort reading the others.
- Real experience with relational data modeling and query performance. You should
  be able to explain why a query is slow, not just that it is.
- Experience running untrusted or semi-trusted workloads — containers, sandboxes,
  job queues, resource isolation. If you have not done exactly this, tell us what
  is closest.
- Judgment about security and privacy that shows up in design decisions, not only
  in code review comments.
- The ability to write clearly. We are a distributed team and most decisions get
  made in writing.

## Nice to have, genuinely optional

- Built or operated a code execution or CI sandbox before.
- Worked at an early-stage company where the roadmap changed under you.
- Have been through a technical hiring loop recently enough to remember how it
  feels from the candidate side.

You do not need a computer science degree, and you do not need to have worked at a
recognizable company. We will evaluate what you can do.

## How we interview

We run the process we would want to sit through, and we tell you every stage up
front. No surprise rounds.

1. **Application review** — a real person reads it. Everyone gets a reply.
2. **Intro call, 30 minutes** — your background, what we are building, your
   questions. No trick questions.
3. **Technical conversation, 60 minutes** — a discussion of a system close to what
   you would build here. Collaborative, no whiteboard algorithm puzzles.
4. **Practical exercise** — a scoped take-home, timeboxed to three hours, and
   **we pay you for it**. Or, if you prefer, we will walk through something you
   have already built instead.
5. **Team conversation, 45 minutes** — working style, expectations both ways.
6. **Offer** — with the band, the reasoning, and time to think.

The whole loop runs in two to three weeks. You get feedback at every stage,
including if we say no — which, given what we do for a living, is the least we
can do.
